# Differential levelling: rise-and-fall method

`surveying.levelling.rise_and_fall` · version 1

Reduces a levelling field book of backsight and foresight pairs from a starting benchmark by the rise-and-fall method: the rise or fall of each setup (backsight − foresight), the elevation of each point sighted, the sums of backsights and foresights and their difference. For a field book of backsight and foresight pairs it gives the same elevations as the height-of-instrument method; the working differs. Closure and adjustment are a separate calculation.

## Inputs

| Name | Meaning | Dimension | Unit | Required | Domain (SI) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `start_elevation` | Elevation of the starting benchmark (below datum is allowed) | L | m | yes | any |
| `backsights` | Staff reading on the previous point, one per setup | list of L | m | yes | ≥ 0 |
| `foresights` | Staff reading on the next point, one per setup | list of L | m | yes | ≥ 0 |

**List group `setups`** (one entry per setup, at least 1): `backsights`, `foresights`. Instrument setups in order of observation. Setup k reads a backsight on the previous point (the start benchmark for setup 1) and a foresight on the next point.

## Outputs

| Name | Meaning | Dimension | Unit | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rises` | Rise of each setup, max(BS − FS, 0) | L | m | list: one per setup (length of `setups`) |
| `falls` | Fall of each setup, max(FS − BS, 0) | L | m | list: one per setup (length of `setups`) |
| `elevations` | Elevation of the point sighted by each setup's foresight, previous elevation + rise − fall | L | m | list: one per setup (length of `setups`) |
| `sum_backsights` | Sum of the backsights, ΣBS | L | m | scalar |
| `sum_foresights` | Sum of the foresights, ΣFS | L | m | scalar |
| `net_rise` | ΣBS − ΣFS: positive when the last point is higher than the start (equals last elevation − start) | L | m | scalar |

## Method

The steps of the calculation, in order, as its trace records them (worked example below):

1. `for each setup: rise = max(BS − FS, 0)`
2. `for each setup: fall = max(FS − BS, 0)`
3. `for each setup: elevation = previous elevation + rise − fall`
4. `ΣBS`
5. `ΣFS`
6. `net rise = ΣBS − ΣFS`

## Validity and limits

- Every input is checked against its mathematical domain (table above); a value outside it is an `OUT_OF_DOMAIN` error.
- Validity range: none is stated, because no reliable source for one has been established. The mathematical domain of every input is still enforced.

## Assumptions

- The field book is a chain of setups, each with one backsight and one foresight; every foresight is to a point whose elevation is wanted (a benchmark or a change point). Intermediate sights are not supported.
- Staff readings are taken from a staff held upright on the point, and are not negative.
- The instrument is in adjustment and the line of sight is level; collimation, curvature and refraction errors are not modelled.
- Elevations are relative to the datum of the start benchmark.

## References

- US Navy, Engineering Aid, NAVEDTRA 14069, chapter 14 (level computations): the field book of Fig. 14-14 (pp. 14-13 to 14-15) and the single-setup example of p. 14-10. Public domain.
- The method is the standard textbook procedure. The cited source reduces its field books by the height-of-instrument method, so the reference cases check numerical consistency with those results; they are not independent validation of this method by a source (D-20 condition 2).
